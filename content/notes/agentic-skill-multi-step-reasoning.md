When an AI agent must move beyond simple retrieval or classification to handle a problem that requires intermediate judgments, Multi-Step Reasoning is the agentic skill that decomposes the problem and chains inference steps toward a conclusion or actionable direction.

It can break a complex question into smaller steps, connect evidence and constraints across those steps, and produce intermediate results that support a final answer, diagnosis, comparison, or course of action.

Its value lies in handling structured, layered, or multi-constraint tasks more systematically than a single direct response, especially when the required logic is too long or complex for one-step generation.

However, the quality of its reasoning depends on the clarity of the problem, the reliability of the inputs, and the validity of the assumptions used in each step, so outputs may still contain hidden errors or unjustified leaps.

In human–AI work, this skill is often used to prepare analyses, clarify options, or draft preliminary conclusions, while the professional remains responsible for validating the logic, interpreting contextual significance, and making the final decision.
