# 1. Working with Agentic AI: Presentation Designer

*How human skills and agentic skills are dynamically integrated and coordinated across presentation design responsibilities*

## 1.1. Presentation Designer as an Occupation

A **presentation designer** transforms complex information, strategies, and data into compelling visual narratives, slide decks, and multimedia presentations. This occupation connects raw organizational knowledge with visual communication by structuring arguments, designing layouts, and applying typographic and graphical elements to support persuasion, education, and decision-making. To ensure these presentations achieve their intended impact, the designer must understand how audience psychology, narrative flow, brand guidelines, data visualization principles, and accessibility standards collectively determine the effectiveness of the final output.

Typical responsibilities of a presentation designer include:

*   audience analysis and narrative strategy;
*   content structuring and storyboarding;
*   visual design and layout generation;
*   data visualization and information design;
*   animation and multimedia integration;
*   template and brand asset management;
*   quality assurance and accessibility compliance; and
*   stakeholder review and iteration management.

These responsibilities are fulfilled through distinct tasks and subtasks, each requiring one or more actions with specific skill requirements. Human skills and agentic skills both contribute to these responsibilities, but their levels and forms of involvement differ according to the responsibility, task, action, content type, brand context, technical environment, and expected outcome.

## 1.2. Human Skills and Agentic Skills in Presentation Design

Presentation design increasingly integrates both **human skills** and **agentic skills**.

Human skills are reusable capabilities possessed and developed by presentation designers. They include narrative strategy, audience empathy, visual hierarchy, spatial reasoning, typographic judgment, brand interpretation, color theory, data storytelling, stakeholder communication, and aesthetic evaluation.

Agentic skills are reusable capabilities available to AI agents through their underlying models, contextual information, instructions, constraints, tools, and computational resources. In presentation design, these skills include text summarization, layout generation, image sourcing, asset resizing, color palette extraction, chart generation, accessibility scanning, template application, text formatting, and file conversion.

Both skill types contribute to the same occupational responsibilities, but their involvement varies by action. Understanding a client's core message relies primarily on human empathy and strategic reasoning; an AI agent then uses agentic skills to summarize background research and extract key themes that support the designer’s narrative. In visual design, the designer first establishes the brand guidelines and visual hierarchy, and an AI agent subsequently generates initial layout variations and sources relevant imagery. For final delivery, an AI agent resizes and exports assets for multiple formats, while the designer reviews the final outputs to ensure brand consistency and emotional impact. Across these actions, human and agentic skills are activated sequentially or iteratively based on task requirements, with the human professional maintaining oversight and final judgment.

The relationship between these skills is not a fixed division where certain responsibilities permanently belong to humans and others to AI. Instead, human and agentic involvement shifts across tasks and between different actions within the same task.

## 1.3. Dynamic Human–Agentic Skill Integration and Collaboration in Presentation Design

Presentation design can be understood as an occupation in which responsibilities are fulfilled through tasks and subtasks, tasks and subtasks are executed through actions, and actions require appropriate configurations of human and agentic skills.

An **occupation** provides the overall professional context. For presentation design, this context encompasses translating complex information into visual narratives, developing and maintaining brand templates, designing data visualizations, and supporting organizational communication.

A **responsibility** represents an expected area of professional work or an outcome that the presentation designer accomplishes or maintains. Responsibilities such as narrative strategy, visual design, data visualization, and accessibility compliance each contain multiple tasks.

A **task** is a **context-dependent unit of work** used to fulfill part or all of a responsibility. When a task is too broad or complex for reliable execution, it is decomposed into smaller **subtasks**.

Tasks and subtasks are executed through one or more **actions**. Actions represent **reusable and configurable operations** such as summarizing a whitepaper, generating a slide layout, applying a master template, formatting a data chart, checking color contrast, or exporting a multimedia file.

Each action has **skill requirements**. These requirements describe the capabilities needed for effective and reliable execution. Depending on the action and its context, human skills, agentic skills, or an integration of both can satisfy these requirements.

The skills available for presentation design reside in a **skill space** containing both human skills and agentic skills. Relevant skills in this space serve as candidate skills for executing the actions required by a particular task. A human skill in narrative strategy, for example, serves as a candidate for structuring a pitch deck, while an agentic skill in text summarization serves as a candidate for condensing source material.

Candidate skills are evaluated relative to the requirements of the action and its occupational and task context. Appropriate skills are then selected and organized into a **skill configuration** for execution. Depending on these requirements, a configuration relies mainly on human skills, mainly on agentic skills, or on an integrated arrangement of both.

### 1.3.1. Skills Competition and Skills Collaboration

Two important relationships can occur between human skills and agentic skills when constructing a skill configuration: **skills competition** and **skills collaboration**.

**Skills competition** occurs when both a human skill and an agentic skill can satisfy the same or similar requirements for an action, but simultaneous application of both is unnecessary. Under a particular occupational and task context, one skill type provides a better fit for the action than the other.

For example, both a presentation designer and an AI agent can generate a standard title slide for an internal meeting. When the brand guidelines, text limits, and layout rules are clearly specified in a master template, the agentic skill provides instant and consistent execution. When the presentation requires a custom, highly conceptual visual metaphor to open a high-stakes investor pitch, the designer's human skills provide the necessary creative and strategic judgment.

The purpose of skills competition is not to establish whether human or agentic skills are generally superior. Rather, it identifies which available skill provides the better fit for a particular action under particular conditions.

**Skills collaboration** occurs when one skill type alone cannot effectively satisfy the requirements of a task or its actions, or when combining complementary human and agentic skills produces stronger task performance.

Collaboration does not require human and agentic skills to operate simultaneously. Instead, they are activated at different stages of action execution in a **sequential, iterative, or recursive manner** according to evolving task requirements.

For example, a designer first identifies that a complex corporate restructuring needs to be explained to non-technical investors. An AI agent then summarizes the restructuring documents and proposes a chronological timeline of events. The designer subsequently evaluates the timeline, determines that a metaphorical visual approach is more persuasive, and sketches the core concept. Based on the designer's direction, the agent next generates vector assets and background variations to support the metaphor. Finally, the designer assembles the final slide, adjusting typography and spacing to ensure maximum persuasive impact. Throughout this sequence, human and agentic skills are activated iteratively as task requirements evolve, while the designer directs the creative process and evaluates its outcomes.

This dynamic relationship allows presentation designers to leverage the complementary strengths of both skill types rather than relying on a permanent allocation of work.

### 1.3.2. Skill Configuration and Task Execution

A **skill configuration** specifies which human skills and agentic skills are used to execute the actions associated with a task or subtask and how those skills are coordinated.

The configuration depends on factors such as action requirements, brand strictness, narrative complexity, data density, audience expectations, available visual assets, accessibility standards, and intermediate stakeholder feedback.

For one action, an agentic skill may independently satisfy the requirements. For another, a human skill may provide the better fit. A complex task may require several configurations across multiple actions, with human and agentic skills repeatedly interacting as the task progresses.

The configuration is therefore dynamic rather than permanent. It changes when the action changes, new stakeholder feedback becomes available, an agentic result fails to satisfy brand requirements, or human interpretation identifies a stronger narrative direction.

Throughout this process, the presentation designer remains the professional responsible for directing and controlling the work associated with the occupation and its responsibilities. Agentic AI provides powerful complementary capabilities that expand what the professional can summarize, format, generate, visualize, animate, and export.

### 1.3.3. Task Performance Evaluation and Skill Space Adaptation

The performance produced through a selected skill configuration is evaluated against **predefined evaluation metrics**. These metrics determine whether the human skills, agentic skills, or their integrated configuration are sufficiently capable of performing the relevant task and its required actions.

Evaluation criteria depend on the work being performed. In presentation design, they include narrative clarity, brand consistency, visual hierarchy, data accuracy, accessibility compliance, typographic correctness, file optimization, and stakeholder alignment.

When a selected skill or skill configuration produces performance above the defined threshold, it passes the performance evaluation and is retained for future applications under appropriate conditions. Repeated successful use provides additional evidence about where that skill or configuration performs effectively.

When performance falls below the required threshold, the corresponding skill or configuration is not reused without modification. The relevant human or agentic skills are refined or reconfigured, existing skills are combined differently, or new human and agentic skills are developed or constructed from scratch when the existing skill space lacks adequate capability.

For example, an AI agent first generates a series of slide layouts based on a new brand guideline, but repeatedly produces text boxes that violate the brand's minimum margin requirements. Task performance evaluation identifies this margin violation as falling below the required threshold. The agentic skill is then refined through stricter spatial constraints, updated bounding-box rules, or access to the brand's master grid template. The revised skill is evaluated again, and once it satisfies the required performance criteria, it is retained for appropriate future layout tasks. Human skills follow a corresponding development process: when a designer lacks the knowledge required to design for a newly adopted immersive display format, the relevant capability is developed through learning, experimentation, and practical experience before its performance is evaluated through subsequent work.

Refined and newly constructed skills that satisfy the required performance criteria are added to the skill space. In this way, task performance evaluation provides feedback not only for completing current work but also for continuously optimizing and expanding the available skill space.

The skill space is not constructed as an isolated collection of occupation-specific representations. Many human and agentic skills used in presentation design are reusable across occupations. Text summarization, layout generation, data visualization, color theory, accessibility checking, image sourcing, and file conversion contribute to work in graphic design, marketing, user experience design, corporate communications, education, and many other fields.

Reusable skills can therefore be represented once and configured differently according to the responsibilities, tasks, actions, and contexts of different occupations. This allows the skill space to serve a growing range of occupations without unnecessarily duplicating or exploding the number of skill representations. Occupational specialization is introduced through task context, action requirements, parameters, brand constraints, and skill configurations rather than by creating a completely separate skill representation whenever the same underlying capability appears in another occupation.

The resulting process is adaptive:

**occupational responsibility → task and subtask → actions → skill requirements → candidate human and agentic skills → skill configuration → task execution → task performance evaluation → skill refinement, reconfiguration, or construction → optimized and expanded skill space**

This cycle allows human and agentic capabilities to evolve alongside the requirements of presentation design work.


## 1.4. Essential Responsibilities of Presentation Designer

### 1.4.1. Audience Analysis and Narrative Strategy

Audience analysis and narrative strategy establishes the core message, emotional tone, and logical flow required to persuade or inform a specific target audience. This responsibility includes understanding stakeholder objectives, analyzing audience demographics and knowledge levels, defining the central argument, and structuring the overarching story.

Agentic skills contribute extensively to background research, document summarization, competitor presentation analysis, and theme extraction. Human skills remain essential for empathizing with the audience, interpreting unstated strategic goals, crafting the emotional arc of the narrative, and deciding which arguments will be most persuasive.

For example, a designer first defines the target audience and the strategic objective for an upcoming investor pitch. An AI agent then gathers and summarizes relevant market research, competitor positioning documents, and historical financial reports. The designer subsequently synthesizes this information to craft a core narrative thesis and opening emphasis or narrative hook. Based on the designer's thesis, the agent next generates several potential narrative arcs and supporting evidence clusters. Finally, the designer selects and refines the most compelling narrative structure to guide the subsequent design phases. This sequence combines human strategic empathy and narrative judgment with agentic research, summarization, and structural organization.

| Human skills                                                                                              | Agentic skills                                                                                                 | Integrated collaboration                                                                                                                                                                      |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| audience empathy, narrative strategy, strategic reasoning, emotional intelligence, stakeholder alignment | text summarization, theme extraction, document analysis, evidence clustering, structural generation | The designer defines the strategic objective and emotional arc, while the AI agent gathers, summarizes, and organizes the background information required to support the narrative. |

### 1.4.2. Content Structuring and Storyboarding

Content structuring and storyboarding transforms the overarching narrative into a slide-by-slide outline, determining the pacing, information density, and visual transitions of the presentation. This responsibility includes breaking down complex text into digestible points, planning visual metaphors, and sequencing the logical flow of information.

Agentic skills are well suited to text condensation, bullet-point generation, outline formatting, and extracting key takeaways from long-form documents. Human skills contribute to determining the optimal pacing, recognizing when information overload occurs, designing visual metaphors, and deciding how to transition between complex ideas.

For example, an AI agent first extracts the core text from a dense technical whitepaper and proposes a preliminary 30-slide outline. The designer reviews the outline and determines that the information density is too high for the intended executive audience. The designer then restructures the flow, reducing the deck to 15 slides and defining a visual metaphor for the core technical process. The agent subsequently populates the revised outline with condensed text and speaker notes based on the designer's new structure. Finally, the designer rewrites the key slide headlines for maximum impact and finalizes the storyboard. Human and agentic skills interact iteratively as the content progresses from raw text to a structured visual plan.

| Human skills                                                                                                 | Agentic skills                                                                                             | Integrated collaboration                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| information pacing, visual metaphor design, content prioritization, copywriting, structural reasoning | text condensation, outline generation, speaker note drafting, keyword extraction, sequence formatting | The AI agent accelerates the extraction and condensation of raw text, while the designer determines the optimal pacing, visual metaphors, and narrative flow for the target audience. |

### 1.4.3. Visual Design and Layout Generation

Visual design and layout generation applies brand guidelines, typography, color theory, and spatial reasoning to create visually compelling and highly readable slides. This responsibility includes designing custom layouts, selecting imagery, establishing visual hierarchy, and ensuring aesthetic consistency across the entire deck.

Agentic skills support layout variation generation, image sourcing, background removal, color palette application, and bulk formatting. Human skills are essential for establishing the creative direction, judging aesthetic balance, interpreting brand guidelines in novel contexts, and ensuring the visual design reinforces the narrative strategy.

For example, a designer first establishes the grid system, typographic hierarchy, and creative direction for a new presentation. An AI agent then generates five distinct layout variations for a complex comparison slide, sourcing relevant stock imagery and applying the brand color palette. The designer reviews the options, selects the strongest layout, and manually refines the spacing and visual weight to achieve perfect balance. Based on the designer's refined master layout, the agent subsequently applies the design logic across the remaining 40 slides in the deck. Finally, the designer performs a final aesthetic review to ensure visual consistency and impact.

| Human skills                                                                                                               | Agentic skills                                                                                    | Integrated collaboration                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| visual hierarchy, spatial reasoning, typographic judgment, aesthetic evaluation, brand interpretation | layout generation, image sourcing, background removal, bulk formatting, color application | The AI agent accelerates the generation of layout variations and bulk application of design rules, while the designer establishes the creative direction and refines the aesthetic balance of the final output. |

### 1.4.4. Data Visualization and Information Design

Data visualization and information design transforms raw numbers and complex relationships into intuitive charts, graphs, and infographics. This responsibility includes selecting the appropriate chart type, simplifying complex datasets, highlighting key insights, and ensuring data accuracy and visual clarity.

Agentic skills efficiently parse spreadsheets, generate standard charts, calculate delta values, and format data labels. Human skills are necessary for identifying the core insight within the data, selecting the most persuasive visualization method, removing chart clutter, and applying visual emphasis to guide the audience's attention.

For example, an AI agent first parses a complex financial spreadsheet and generates standard bar and line charts for an upcoming quarterly review. The designer reviews the charts and identifies that the most critical insight is the margin growth relative to revenue, which is obscured in the standard formats. The designer then directs the agent to transform the data into a custom waterfall chart and highlights the specific delta values. The agent subsequently calculates the exact percentages and generates the updated visualization. Finally, the designer adjusts the color coding and annotations to ensure the audience immediately grasps the strategic takeaway.

| Human skills                                                                                                               | Agentic skills                                                                                    | Integrated collaboration                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| data storytelling, chart selection, insight identification, visual emphasis, clutter reduction | data parsing, chart generation, delta calculation, label formatting, dataset transformation | The AI agent handles the computational parsing and generation of standard charts, while the designer identifies the core insight and designs the custom visualization required to persuade the audience. |

### 1.4.5. Animation and Multimedia Integration

Animation and multimedia integration adds motion, transitions, video, and interactive elements to enhance the presentation experience without distracting from the core message. This responsibility includes designing transition logic, timing animations, embedding media, and ensuring smooth playback across different display environments.

Agentic skills assist with applying baseline morph transitions, generating animation scripts, embedding video files, and optimizing media compression. Human skills are especially important for defining the psychological purpose of the motion, adjusting animation timing and transitions to match the speaker's cadence, and ensuring multimedia elements support rather than overwhelm the narrative.

For example, a designer first defines the transition logic and psychological pacing for a product reveal sequence. An AI agent then applies the baseline morph transitions and embeds the high-resolution product videos across the relevant slides. The designer reviews the sequence and identifies that the transitions are too abrupt for the intended premium brand feel. After the designer adjusts the timing and easing curves, the agent subsequently renders a high-fidelity preview and optimizes the video compression for the target display hardware. Finally, the designer verifies the playback smoothness and finalizes the multimedia integration.

| Human skills                                                                                                        | Agentic skills                                                                                                     | Integrated collaboration                                                                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| motion psychology, timing and pacing, multimedia strategy, aesthetic judgment, user experience design | morph application, media embedding, compression optimization, script generation, preview rendering | The designer defines the purpose and pacing of the motion, while the AI agent executes the technical application, embedding, and optimization of the multimedia elements. |

### 1.4.6. Template and Brand Asset Management

Template and brand asset management creates and maintains reusable master slides, design systems, and brand guidelines that ensure consistency across an organization's presentations. This responsibility includes defining master layouts, setting global typography and color rules, creating custom icon libraries, and updating templates when brand identities evolve.

Agentic skills assist with scanning existing decks for brand compliance, updating hex codes across master slides, generating layout variations based on grid rules, and exporting template packages. Human skills remain important for interpreting new brand guidelines, defining the rules for edge-case layouts, and ensuring the template system is flexible enough for non-designers to use correctly.

For example, an organization first introduces a revised logo and color palette that affects all corporate templates. The designer defines the new spatial rules, typography scales, and approved use cases for the new assets. An AI agent then inspects the existing template structure and updates approved colors, logo placements, and default text styles to match the new guidelines. The designer reviews the updated master deck and resolves edge cases where the new logo disrupts existing complex layouts. Finally, the agent exports the finalized template package and generates a usage guide for the broader organization.

| Human skills                                                                                                   | Agentic skills                                                                                                               | Integrated collaboration                                                                                                                                                                     |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| design system architecture, brand interpretation, edge-case resolution, usability design, guideline creation | hex code updating, master slide scanning, layout variation generation, package exporting, compliance checking | The designer defines the structural rules and brand interpretations, while the AI agent executes the bulk technical updates and compliance scanning across the master template system. |

### 1.4.7. Quality Assurance and Accessibility Compliance

Quality assurance and accessibility compliance ensures that presentations are technically sound, visually consistent, and accessible to all users, including those with visual or cognitive impairments. This responsibility includes checking color contrast, verifying reading order for screen readers, ensuring cross-device compatibility, and proofreading for typographic errors.

Agentic skills generate accessibility reports, scan for contrast ratio violations, verify alt-text presence, check reading order logic, and identify broken links or embedded media errors. Human skills define the acceptable thresholds for brand compliance, interpret ambiguous accessibility warnings, and determine the appropriate design corrections.

For example, a designer first completes the final design of a 50-slide public-facing presentation. An AI agent then scans the entire file and generates a comprehensive report detailing contrast ratio violations, missing alt-text on charts, and incorrect reading orders for screen readers. The designer reviews the accessibility report, prioritizes the critical failures, and manually redesigns the flagged elements to maintain aesthetic quality while achieving compliance. The agent subsequently re-scans the revised file to verify that all accessibility thresholds are met. Finally, the designer approves the deck for public distribution.

| Human skills                                                                                   | Agentic skills                                                                                           | Integrated collaboration                                                                                                                                                      |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| accessibility strategy, aesthetic compromise resolution, compliance judgment, proofreading | contrast scanning, alt-text verification, reading order checking, link validation, report generation | The AI agent performs systematic technical and accessibility scanning, while the designer interprets the results and executes the design corrections required for compliance. |

### 1.4.8. Stakeholder Review and Iteration Management

Stakeholder review and iteration management navigates the feedback process, resolving conflicting requests, incorporating strategic changes, and finalizing the presentation for delivery. This responsibility includes gathering feedback, clustering comments, mediating stakeholder disagreements, executing revisions, and managing version control.

Agentic skills summarize stakeholder comments, cluster feedback by theme or slide number, track version changes, and apply standardized text edits across multiple slides. Human skills evaluate the strategic validity of the feedback, resolve conflicting stakeholder requests, protect the narrative integrity of the deck, and communicate design rationale to non-designers.

For example, stakeholders first return a draft presentation with over 40 distinct comments and conflicting strategic requests. An AI agent then clusters the comments by theme, highlights the contradictions, and drafts a summary report for the designer. The designer reviews the report, mediates the conflicting requests with the lead stakeholder, and determines which feedback aligns with the core narrative strategy. Based on the designer's approved revision plan, the agent subsequently applies the standardized text edits and layout adjustments across the deck. Finally, the designer presents the revised version, explaining the design rationale to the stakeholders and securing final approval.

| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| feedback mediation, narrative protection, stakeholder communication, strategic evaluation, version control | comment clustering, contradiction highlighting, bulk text editing, version tracking, summary generation | The AI agent organizes and executes the mechanical aspects of the revision process, while the designer mediates stakeholder conflicts and protects the strategic and narrative integrity of the final presentation.     |

## 1.6. Working as a Presentation Designer with Agentic AI

Agentic AI is becoming an increasingly powerful component of presentation design. Presentation designers should therefore adopt and integrate AI agents as complementary participants in professional workflows rather than treating them only as occasional tools for image sourcing or text summarization.

This shift does not reduce the importance of design expertise. Human professionals remain responsible for the occupation and its corresponding responsibilities. They understand audience psychology, craft compelling narratives, establish visual hierarchies, interpret brand guidelines, evaluate aesthetic quality, mediate stakeholder feedback, control strategic messaging, evaluate task performance, and decide how human and agentic skills are configured and improved.

At the same time, agentic skills greatly expand the volume and variety of design work that can be processed. AI agents summarize extensive research documents, generate multiple layout variations, parse complex datasets into charts, scan hundreds of slides for accessibility compliance, apply bulk formatting changes, and optimize multimedia assets at a scale difficult to achieve through direct human effort alone.

Strong presentation design practice is therefore not based on maximizing human work or agentic work independently. It is based on finding appropriate configurations of human skills and agentic skills for the responsibilities, tasks, subtasks, and actions being performed.

Where a human skill and an agentic skill can independently perform the same action, skills competition identifies which is better suited to the current context. Where work requires complementary capabilities, skills collaboration results in human and agentic skills being activated sequentially, iteratively, or recursively to achieve stronger task performance than either skill type achieves alone.

Task performance evaluation then determines whether selected skills and configurations satisfy predefined expectations. Successful skills are retained and reused, while inadequate skills are refined, reconfigured, or newly developed. The resulting capabilities return to an increasingly useful skill space that supports presentation design and, where capabilities are reusable, other occupations as well.

For a presentation designer, working effectively with agentic AI means treating AI as a powerful complementary capability integrated into everyday professional routines while maintaining human control of occupational responsibilities. Through appropriate skill selection, competition, collaboration, evaluation, and continuous improvement, human professionals and agentic AI work together to achieve presentation design that is more efficient, scalable, adaptable, persuasive, and accessible.
