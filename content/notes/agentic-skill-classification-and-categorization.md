**Classification & Categorization**, as an agentic skill, is the capability of an AI agent to assign items such as documents, records, messages, images, transactions, products, incidents, or data points to predefined or discovered categories, labels, codes, classes, or taxonomies.

It operates by examining features, meanings, patterns, metadata, or contextual signals in the input and matching them to category definitions, rules, learned models, or similarity structures.

The skill can support activities such as routing cases to the correct queue, tagging content for retrieval, coding observations for analysis, grouping customers or risks, and preparing labeled data for reporting or downstream modeling.

It is different from merely organizing content into tables, outlines, or schemas, because its primary output is a category assignment rather than a new arrangement of the material.

In practice, classification may be deterministic, rule-based, probabilistic, or based on machine-learned patterns, and it can handle large volumes of items more consistently and rapidly than manual review.

However, category boundaries can be ambiguous, overlapping, or dependent on domain context, and an AI agent may misclassify unusual, incomplete, or subtly phrased cases.

Therefore, this skill is most reliable when category definitions are clear, when confidence or uncertainty can be reported, and when human professionals review borderline, high-stakes, or conceptually complex assignments.
