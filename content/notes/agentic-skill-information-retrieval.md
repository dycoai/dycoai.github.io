**Information Retrieval**, as an **agentic skill**, is the capability of an AI agent to search, locate, and gather relevant information from documents, databases, web sources, and other digital repositories in response to a task-defined need.

It involves formulating queries, navigating source collections, identifying candidate materials, and returning evidence that can support later processing such as summarization, extraction, comparison, or reasoning. It is similar to a research assistant that can look through many sources quickly, but it remains a technical AI capability because the agent uses model-based understanding, retrieval mechanisms, filters, and tool access to find information.

Its value lies in speed, scale, and repeatability, especially when the task requires scanning large volumes of material or updating evidence from changing sources. At the same time, retrieval is not the same as understanding, verification, or decision-making: the agent may collect plausible material without knowing which facts are ultimately important, accurate, or contextually appropriate.

Therefore, in human–AI work, this skill can be used alone for simple lookup tasks, or it can be combined with human skills such as domain knowledge, critical reasoning, and analytical judgment, where the agent gathers sources and the human evaluates relevance, credibility, and significance.

In action-based skill selection, where skills are chosen according to the specific action a task requires, Information Retrieval is chosen when the immediate requirement is to find and assemble evidence rather than interpret it, and it may either substitute for manual searching or support a larger workflow in which subsequent actions rely on the retrieved material.
