Before an AI agent can retrieve, extract, summarize, or act upon any textual input, it must first parse and understand what that input means, and this Natural Language Comprehension is the agentic skill that performs this foundational act of sense-making.

It enables an agent to interpret unstructured language—including domain-specific jargon, technical terminology, and ambiguous or incomplete phrasing—and derive coherent meaning, intent, and contextual nuance from raw text.

Its value lies in bridging the gap between the variability of natural human expression and the structured operations that follow, serving as a prerequisite for nearly all downstream information processing.

However, comprehension alone does not condense content into summaries, pull structured fields from documents, or generate new text; it establishes understanding without transforming the material further.

In human–AI work, this skill allows professionals to communicate instructions, requirements, and context in ordinary language while the agent interprets them accurately enough to proceed with subsequent actions.
