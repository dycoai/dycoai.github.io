# 1. Dynamic Human–Agentic Skill Integration and Collaboration (DyCo Skills)

## 1.1. Introducing DyCo Skills, a Dynamic Human–Agentic Skill Integration and Collaboration Framework

Contemporary professional work increasingly requires capabilities that extend beyond the human skills possessed by individual professionals. As occupational responsibilities become more complex, information-intensive, and technology-mediated, relying exclusively on human skills may limit the effectiveness, efficiency, adaptability, and scalability with which professional work can be performed. At the same time, advances in large language models and other foundation models have enabled AI agents to possess or access a growing range of **agentic skills**, including information retrieval, extraction, classification, summarization, comparison, calculation, generation, reasoning support, and tool use. The central challenge is therefore not simply whether AI agents can perform professional work, but how human skills and agentic skills can be dynamically and flexibly selected, configured, integrated, and coordinated according to the requirements of particular professional activities. To address this challenge, **Dynamic Human–Agentic Skill Integration and Collaboration (DyCo Skills)** is proposed as a theoretical framework for organizing the dynamic integration of human and agentic capabilities within occupational work under the control and oversight of the human professional.

DyCo Skills conceptualizes occupational work as a structured but adaptable system in which an **occupation** establishes the overall professional context and is associated with a characteristic set of **responsibilities**. Each responsibility represents a professional obligation or expected outcome and is operationalized through one or more context-dependent **tasks**. When a task is not sufficiently executable at its current level of abstraction, it can be decomposed into smaller **subtasks** until the resulting units of work can be executed with an acceptable expected level of effectiveness and reliability. Tasks and subtasks are subsequently realized through one or more **actions**, which represent reusable and configurable operations. Whereas tasks and subtasks are defined by their particular occupational and work contexts, actions provide a reusable operational layer that can be instantiated with specific inputs, parameters, constraints, and expectations.

Actions constitute the central connection between the work to be accomplished and the capabilities available to accomplish it. Each action has **skill requirements** that specify the capabilities necessary for its effective and reliable execution. These requirements may be satisfied by **human skills**, **agentic skills**, or an appropriate integration of both. Human skills represent reusable capabilities possessed and developed by human professionals, including professional knowledge, reasoning, judgment, communication, creativity, and physical capabilities. Agentic skills represent reusable capabilities available to AI agents through their underlying models, contextual information, constraints, tools, and other computational resources. DyCo Skills therefore separates the definition of an action from the capabilities used to execute it: the same reusable action may be performed through different skill configurations depending on its context-aware requirements and the capabilities currently available.

To support this dynamic assignment, DyCo Skills represents human skills and agentic skills within a common **Skill Space**. Rather than treating human professionals and AI agents as fixed substitutes or predetermined collaborators, the Skill Space provides a shared representational basis for comparing their capabilities relative to the skill requirements of actions. Skills with similar capabilities may satisfy similar action requirements, while differences between them represent distinct strengths, limitations, and performance characteristics. These relationships can be evaluated through predefined metrics, allowing the framework to search for and compare candidate human and agentic skills according to the particular action being executed. The resulting action–skill relationship provides the foundation for determining where human capabilities, agentic capabilities, or their integration are most appropriate.

Within this process, human and agentic skills may exhibit either **competitive or collaborative relationships**. Competition occurs when multiple available skills provide alternative means of satisfying the same or similar requirements and their simultaneous application is unnecessary. In such cases, context-aware skill selection determines which available skill or set of skills is most appropriate according to the action, its associated task and responsibility, relevant constraints and expectations, and predefined evaluation metrics. Collaboration occurs when the requirements of an action are better satisfied through complementary capabilities, particularly when human and agentic skills contribute different but mutually supporting functions. Competition and collaboration are therefore not fixed relationships between humans and AI agents; rather, they emerge dynamically from the relationship between available capabilities and the context-aware skill requirements of each action.

The selected skills are subsequently organized into a **skill configuration** that specifies which capabilities will be applied and how they will be assigned, parameterized, integrated, and coordinated. Consequently, different actions within the same task may use substantially different configurations. One action may primarily depend on human skills, another may be delegated to agentic skills under human oversight, and another may require close integration and collaboration between human and agentic skills. **Dynamic integration** operationalizes these configurations during execution, allowing the division and coordination of capabilities to change according to the characteristics and requirements of each action rather than imposing a fixed allocation of work between the human professional and the AI agent. The primary interest of DyCo Skills is this dynamic integration and collaboration of complementary human and agentic skills while maintaining the human professional as the controlling and overseeing professional actor.

DyCo Skills also treats skill availability and capability as **adaptive rather than static**. Human skills may be acquired, developed, practiced, and refined, while agentic skills may be constructed, configured, combined, adapted, or otherwise improved through their supporting models, contextual resources, tools, and computational mechanisms. When available capabilities do not adequately satisfy the requirements of an action, existing skills may be enhanced or reconfigured, or new skills may be developed and verified. Skills that satisfy predefined criteria for effectiveness, reliability, and reusability can subsequently be incorporated into the Skill Space. In this way, the framework accommodates changes not only in occupational responsibilities and tasks but also in the capabilities available for performing them.

Finally, **execution evaluation** closes the adaptive cycle of the framework. The outcome of each executed action is assessed against predefined metrics and expectations to determine whether the applied human skills, agentic skills, or their integrated configuration produced an effective and reliable result. Evaluation is therefore concerned not only with accepting or revising an immediate outcome but also with generating feedback for subsequent skill assignment, configuration, integration, and development. Unsatisfactory performance may lead to a revised skill configuration, refinement of existing capabilities, or construction and development of new capabilities, while successful configurations and skills can be retained and reused in subsequent actions. These evaluation outcomes progressively inform and optimize the Skill Space and its future use.

Taken together, DyCo Skills provides an **action-centered and human-controlled theoretical framework** for understanding how human and agentic capabilities can be dynamically integrated within occupational work. It connects the professional structure of occupations, responsibilities, tasks, and subtasks with the operational structure of actions, skill requirements, Skill Space representation, action–skill matching, competitive selection, collaborative configuration, dynamic integration, and execution evaluation. Rather than assuming that AI simply replaces human work or that humans and AI must always collaborate, DyCo Skills conceptualizes human–AI collaboration as a **dynamic configuration problem at the skill and action levels**. The appropriate relationship between human and agentic capabilities is determined for each action according to its context and requirements, while the human professional retains control and oversight of the overall process. Through continuous execution, evaluation, and capability development, the framework provides a theoretical basis for adapting human–agentic collaboration as professional responsibilities, work contexts, and available capabilities evolve.

## 1.2. Concept Definitions

### 1.2.1. Occupation

**Occupation.** An occupation is a professional role associated with a predefined characteristic set of responsibilities and professional practices. It describes the general kind of work a person is expected to perform, such as a researcher, teacher, accountant, engineer, or physician. An occupation provides the overall professional context within which responsibilities, tasks, and actions are defined and evolve over time.

### 1.2.2. Responsibility

**Responsibility.** A responsibility is a professional obligation or expected outcome associated with an occupation. It specifies what is expected to be accomplished or maintained by a human professional, potentially in collaboration with agentic AI, rather than the detailed steps for doing so. An occupation contains multiple responsibilities, with each responsibility fulfilled through one or more tasks.

### 1.2.3. Task and Subtask

**Task and Subtask.** A task is a context-dependent unit of work performed to fulfill part or all of a responsibility. A task may be divided into smaller subtasks when finer decomposition is required for better execution of the task and better application of human skills and agentic skills. Whether a task requires further decomposition is determined through predefined evaluation metrics that assess whether the task can be assigned and executed with an acceptable expected level of effectiveness and reliability using available skills. A task that satisfies these metrics is sufficiently executable and does not require further decomposition; otherwise, it is decomposed into subtasks that are evaluated in the same manner.

### 1.2.4. Action with Skill Requirements

**Action with Skill Requirements.** An action is a reusable and configurable operation used, individually or in combination with other actions, to execute a task or subtask. An action is defined independently of a particular task context and becomes context-specific when it is applied with particular inputs, parameters, constraints, and expectations. Each action has skill requirements, which specify what human skills and/or agentic skills are required to perform the action effectively and reliably.

### 1.2.5. Human Professional and AI Agent

**Human Professional and AI Agent.** A human professional is a person who performs work within an occupation and applies professional skills that are learned, refined, and evolved over time to fulfill assigned responsibilities. An AI agent is an artificial system, mainly supported by large language models or other foundation models, that uses its parametric capabilities, contextual information, constraints, and tools to perform assigned actions toward specified objectives. Human professionals and AI agents can be dynamically integrated to collaborate in executing actions within the same workflow to realize the expected outcomes.

### 1.2.6. Human Skills and Agentic Skills

**Human Skills and Agentic Skills.** **Human skills** are reusable capabilities possessed by human professionals and may include innate capabilities and acquired capabilities that are learned, refined, and evolved over time, such as domain knowledge, reasoning, communication, judgment, creativity, and physical abilities. **Agentic skills** are reusable capabilities available to an AI agent, supported by its parametric capabilities, contextual information, constraints, and tools, such as information retrieval, extraction, classification, summarization, comparison, calculation, generation, and tool use. Human skills and agentic skills can be dynamically assigned and configured according to the skill requirements of the actions required to execute a specific task or subtask. **The actions required by a task or subtask may therefore be performed by a human professional, an AI agent, or an integration of both,** depending on whether their respective skills satisfy the skill requirements of the required actions for effective and reliable execution.

### 1.2.7. Skills Construction and Optimization

**Skills Construction and Optimization.** Skills construction and optimization is the process of developing, evaluating, and continuously refining human skills and agentic skills in the skill space. Human skills and agentic skills follow different construction and development pipelines and methodologies, but both are initially constructed, acquired, or developed to establish the skill space required to support the execution of actions associated with the tasks, subtasks, and responsibilities of an occupation. During the execution of actions, the applied human skills and agentic skills are evaluated against predefined evaluation metrics according to their effectiveness, reliability, and other relevant performance requirements. Skills that satisfy the evaluation criteria are retained in the skill space and reused for subsequent actions, while skills that fail to satisfy the criteria are refined, reconfigured, replaced, or reconstructed as necessary. When existing skills cannot be adequately improved or no suitable skill exists, existing skills may be adapted or combined, or new human or agentic skills may be acquired, developed, or constructed and verified before being incorporated into the skill space. Through repeated execution, evaluation, and refinement, the skill space is continuously optimized and evolved to support increasingly effective and reliable execution of professional responsibilities.

### 1.2.8. Skill Space

**Skill Space.** A skill space is a common representational space containing two sets of reusable capabilities: the set of human skills possessed by human professionals and the set of agentic skills available to AI agents. Human skills and agentic skills are represented and positioned in the skill space according to measurable capability characteristics relevant to satisfying the skill requirements of actions. When a human skill and an agentic skill can perform the same or similar actions, they are positioned close to each other, with greater overlap representing a closer match in capabilities and differences representing their respective strengths and limitations. Their positions and relationships in the skill space can be determined through a set of predefined evaluation metrics, providing a basis for dynamically assigning human skills, agentic skills, or their integration to the actions required to execute a task or subtask effectively and reliably.

### 1.2.9. Competition and Collaboration of Human Skills and Agentic Skills

**Competition and Collaboration of Human Skills and Agentic Skills.** For a specific action required to execute a task or subtask, multiple human skills and agentic skills in the skill space may be capable of satisfying the same or similar skill requirements. When multiple skills can perform the same action but their simultaneous use is unnecessary, they operate in a **competition mode**, in which a context-aware decision is made to select the most appropriate skill based on the characteristics of the action, its associated task and responsibility, relevant constraints and expectations, and predefined evaluation metrics. When an action requires multiple complementary skills to satisfy its skill requirements, particularly when both human skills and agentic skills are needed, they operate in a **collaboration mode**, in which the selected skills are integrated and coordinated for joint execution. Skill searching, action–skill matching, and skill selection therefore provide the mechanisms for identifying candidate skills, evaluating their suitability, and determining the final skill configuration from the skill space for effective and reliable execution of each action.

### 1.2.10. Skill Configuration

**Skill Configuration.** Skill configuration is the context-aware specification of the human skills and/or agentic skills selected from the skill space and the arrangement in which they are assigned, parameterized, integrated, and coordinated to satisfy the context-aware skill requirements of a specific action. A skill configuration may consist of a single human skill, a single agentic skill, multiple human or agentic skills, or an integration of human and agentic skills, depending on the characteristics of the action, its associated task and responsibility, relevant constraints and expectations, and predefined predefined evaluation metrics. When multiple skills are selected, the configuration specifies their respective roles, execution relationships, and coordination required for effective and reliable execution of the action. The resulting skill configuration provides the executable arrangement of capabilities through which the action is performed by a human professional, an AI agent, or through human–AI collaboration.

### 1.2.11. Dynamic Integration for Collaboration

**Dynamic Integration for Collaboration.** **Dynamic integration for collaboration** is the process of matching the skill requirements of actions with available human skills and agentic skills in the skill space and dynamically assigning or coordinating their execution accordingly. An action may be executed by a human professional, an AI agent, or through human–AI collaboration, depending on the required skills, context, constraints, and expected effectiveness and reliability. This enables human and agentic capabilities to be integrated differently across tasks while working toward the same professional responsibilities. When the skill requirements of an action cannot be adequately satisfied by the available skills in the skill space, new skills may be constructed from existing skills or developed from scratch and evaluated through predefined metrics. If a new skill is verified to be effective, reliable, and reusable, it is incorporated into the skill space as a new member for subsequent use.
### 1.2.12. Execution Evaluation

**Execution Evaluation.** Execution evaluation is the process of assessing the outcome of an executed action against predefined metrics and expectations to determine whether the action has been performed effectively and reliably by the integrated collaboration between human skills and agentic skills or individual skills that under the control and oversight of the human professional. The evaluation results provide feedback for accepting or revising the outcome, reassigning or reconfiguring task-aware skill configuration, enhancing the existing human and agentic skills, and develop new human and agentic skills to optimize and expand the skill space.
