# 1. Working with Agentic AI: Business Execution Consultant

_How human skills and agentic skills can be dynamically integrated and coordinated across business execution responsibilities_

## 1.1. Business Execution Consultant as an Occupation

A **business execution consultant** is a professional who helps organizations translate business objectives, strategies, and improvement opportunities into coordinated initiatives, operational changes, requirements, and measurable outcomes. The occupation connects strategic planning with business analysis, process improvement, implementation, risk management, performance measurement, stakeholder coordination, and organizational change.

Business execution consulting extends beyond planning projects or documenting business requirements. A business execution consultant must understand how organizational objectives, business processes, operating procedures, technology, stakeholder needs, risks, policies, performance measures, dependencies, and implementation constraints interact. The consultant analyzes business challenges, develops executable approaches, coordinates activities across organizational functions, monitors progress, and helps ensure that intended business outcomes are realized.

Typical responsibilities of a business execution consultant include:

- strategic planning and business initiative execution;
    
- business problem analysis and requirements definition;
    
- process improvement and root-cause analysis;
    
- implementation, change, and project lifecycle coordination;
    
- risk, compliance, governance, and execution control;
    
- business performance measurement and outcome realization; and
    
- stakeholder coordination, communication, and user enablement.
    

These responsibilities are fulfilled through different tasks and subtasks, and each task may require multiple actions with different skill requirements. Human skills and agentic skills can both contribute to performing these responsibilities, but their levels and forms of involvement may differ according to the complexity of the initiative, task ambiguity, stakeholder diversity, information quality, risk exposure, technical dependencies, organizational consequences, and expected outcome.

## 1.2. Human Skills and Agentic Skills in Business Execution Consulting

Business execution consulting increasingly involves both **human skills** and **agentic skills**.

Human skills are reusable capabilities possessed and developed by business execution consultants. They may include strategic reasoning, business analysis, requirements elicitation, process analysis, root-cause reasoning, project coordination, risk judgment, stakeholder management, facilitation, negotiation, organizational understanding, communication, problem solving, and professional judgment.

Agentic skills are reusable capabilities available to AI agents through their underlying models, contextual information, instructions, constraints, tools, and computational resources. In business execution consulting, these skills may include information retrieval, document extraction, summarization, requirements drafting, process comparison, data analysis, metric calculation, root-cause support, scenario analysis, risk identification, status reporting, issue tracking, document generation, and tool use.

Both skill types can contribute to the same occupational responsibilities, but their involvement levels vary. Strategic planning may depend strongly on human understanding of organizational priorities while using agentic skills to organize evidence and compare alternatives. Requirements work may involve substantial agentic drafting and consistency checking while relying on human skills for elicitation and interpretation. Performance monitoring may rely heavily on agentic analysis of metrics while requiring human judgment to determine whether results indicate a real business problem or require corrective action.

The useful relationship is therefore not a fixed division in which certain responsibilities permanently belong to humans and others belong to AI. Human and agentic involvement can change from one task to another and even between different actions within the same task.

## 1.3. Dynamic Human–Agentic Skill Integration and Collaboration in Business Execution Consulting

Business execution consulting can be understood as an occupation in which responsibilities are fulfilled through tasks and subtasks, tasks and subtasks are executed through actions, and actions require appropriate configurations of human and agentic skills.

An **occupation** provides the overall professional context. For business execution consulting, this context includes translating business objectives into executable plans, analyzing operational challenges, defining requirements, coordinating implementation, managing execution risks, monitoring outcomes, and supporting organizational adoption.

A **responsibility** represents an expected area of professional work or an outcome that the business execution consultant is expected to accomplish or maintain. Responsibilities such as planning initiatives, defining requirements, improving processes, coordinating change, or monitoring business outcomes may each contain multiple tasks.

A **task** is a context-dependent unit of work used to fulfill part or all of a responsibility. When a task is too broad or complex for reliable execution, it may be decomposed into smaller **subtasks**.

Tasks and subtasks are executed through one or more **actions**. Actions represent reusable and configurable operations such as retrieving information, analyzing a business problem, eliciting requirements, mapping a process, calculating a performance metric, identifying a root cause, documenting a user story, tracking an issue, assessing a risk, facilitating a working session, or preparing an implementation recommendation.

Each action has **skill requirements**. These requirements describe the capabilities needed for the action to be executed effectively and reliably. Depending on the action and its context, the requirements may be satisfied by human skills, agentic skills, or an integration of both.

The skills available for business execution consulting reside in a **skill space** containing both human skills and agentic skills. Some skills in this space become candidate skills for executing the actions required by a particular task. A human skill in stakeholder facilitation, for example, may be a candidate for resolving conflicting requirements, while an agentic skill in document comparison may be a candidate for identifying inconsistencies among requirement artifacts.

The candidate skills are considered relative to the requirements of the action and the occupational and task context. Appropriate skills are then selected and organized into a **skill configuration** for execution. A configuration may rely mainly on human skills, mainly on agentic skills, or on an integrated arrangement of both.

### 1.3.1. Skills Competition and Skills Collaboration

Two important relationships may occur between human skills and agentic skills when a skill configuration is constructed: **skills competition** and **skills collaboration**.

**Skills competition** occurs when both a human skill and an agentic skill can satisfy the same or similar requirements for an action, but both do not need to be applied simultaneously. Under a particular occupational and task context, one may be better suited than the other.

For example, both a business execution consultant and an AI agent may be capable of comparing two documented business processes and identifying differences. When the documentation is complete and the comparison criteria are explicit, an agentic skill may perform the action more quickly and consistently. When important differences depend on informal practices, organizational relationships, or undocumented constraints, the consultant's human understanding may provide the better fit.

The purpose of skills competition is therefore not to establish whether human or agentic skills are generally superior. It is to identify which available skill provides the better fit for a particular action under particular conditions.

**Skills collaboration** occurs when the requirements of a task or its actions cannot be satisfied effectively by one skill type alone, or when combining complementary human and agentic skills is expected to produce better task performance.

Collaboration does not require human and agentic skills to operate at exactly the same time. They may be activated at different stages of action execution in a **sequential, iterative, or recursive manner**.

For example, the consultant may lead a requirements session to understand a business problem, an AI agent may structure the discussion into candidate requirements and unresolved questions, the consultant may return to stakeholders to clarify those questions, the agent may revise the requirements and compare them with existing processes, and the consultant may then determine whether the proposed solution adequately addresses the original problem.

This dynamic relationship allows business execution consultants to exploit the complementary strengths of both skill types rather than relying on a permanent allocation of work.

### 1.3.2. Skill Configuration and Task Execution

A skill configuration specifies which human skills and agentic skills will be used to execute the actions associated with a task or subtask and how those skills will be coordinated.

The configuration depends on factors such as initiative complexity, task ambiguity, stakeholder diversity, information quality, process maturity, technical dependencies, risk exposure, compliance requirements, expected reliability, available tools, delivery deadlines, and intermediate results.

For one action, an agentic skill may independently satisfy the requirements. For another, a human skill may be selected instead. A complex business initiative may require several configurations across multiple actions, with human and agentic skills repeatedly interacting as the work progresses.

The configuration is therefore dynamic rather than permanent. It may change when new stakeholder information becomes available, an assumption proves incorrect, a requirement changes, an implementation risk emerges, an agentic result fails validation, or human interpretation identifies a different execution direction.

Throughout this process, the business execution consultant remains the professional responsible for promoting and controlling the work associated with the occupation and its responsibilities. Agentic AI provides powerful complementary capabilities that can expand what the professional can retrieve, analyze, compare, document, monitor, coordinate, and evaluate.

### 1.3.3. Task Performance Evaluation and Skill Space Adaptation

The performance produced through a selected skill configuration must be evaluated against **predefined evaluation metrics**. These metrics determine whether the human skills, agentic skills, or their integrated configuration are sufficiently capable of performing the relevant task and its required actions.

The evaluation criteria depend on the work being performed. In business execution consulting, they may include requirement quality, analytical accuracy, completeness, process improvement, implementation progress, risk control, stakeholder alignment, business-outcome achievement, timeliness, traceability, compliance, and reliability.

When a selected skill or skill configuration produces performance above the defined threshold, it passes the performance evaluation and can be retained for future applications under appropriate conditions. Repeated successful use provides additional evidence about where that skill or configuration can be applied effectively.

When performance falls below the required threshold, the corresponding skill or configuration should not simply be reused without modification. Human or agentic skills may be refined or reconfigured. Existing skills may also be combined differently, or new human and agentic skills may be developed or constructed when the existing skill space does not provide an adequate capability.

For example, if an agentic skill produces requirements that are consistently well structured but frequently misses dependencies between business processes, the capability may be refined through better contextual information, dependency-analysis procedures, additional source access, or a mandatory human-review stage. If the consultant lacks sufficient expertise in a newly introduced operating model, the corresponding human skill may be developed through research, training, collaboration, and practical experience.

Refined and newly constructed skills that satisfy the required performance criteria can be added to the skill space. Task performance evaluation therefore provides feedback not only for completing current initiatives but also for continuously optimizing and expanding available capabilities.

The skill space should not be constructed as an isolated collection of occupation-specific representations. Many capabilities used in business execution consulting are reusable across occupations. Requirements analysis, process mapping, root-cause reasoning, project coordination, risk analysis, information retrieval, metric calculation, communication, documentation, facilitation, and tool use may also contribute to business analysis, management consulting, project management, operations analysis, product management, risk management, and other professional work.

Reusable skills can therefore be represented once and configured differently according to the responsibilities, tasks, actions, and contexts of different occupations. Occupational specialization can be introduced through organizational context, task requirements, tools, constraints, policies, stakeholder information, parameters, and skill configurations rather than unnecessarily creating a separate representation whenever the same underlying capability appears elsewhere.

The resulting process is adaptive:

**occupational responsibility → task and subtask → actions → skill requirements → candidate human and agentic skills → skill configuration → task execution → task performance evaluation → skill refinement, reconfiguration, or construction → optimized and expanded skill space**

This cycle allows human and agentic capabilities to evolve together with the requirements of business execution consulting.

## 1.4. Essential Responsibilities of Business Execution Consultant

### 1.4.1. Strategic Planning and Business Initiative Execution

Strategic planning and business initiative execution translates organizational objectives into coordinated initiatives, deliverables, priorities, and executable plans. The responsibility may involve contributing to large-scale planning, defining desired outcomes, establishing execution approaches, coordinating priorities, identifying dependencies, and maintaining progress toward business objectives.

Human skills are particularly important for understanding organizational priorities, balancing competing objectives, interpreting strategic context, resolving ambiguity, and determining which execution approach is realistic. Agentic skills can complement this work through information synthesis, dependency analysis, planning support, scenario comparison, scheduling assistance, and status aggregation.

For example, an organization may establish an objective to improve the speed and reliability of an important business process. The consultant works with leaders to define the desired outcome and major constraints. An AI agent can organize current initiatives, identify dependencies, compare implementation sequences, and highlight conflicting milestones. The consultant evaluates the alternatives and determines how the work should be prioritized. As execution proceeds, the agent can continually update progress information while the consultant adjusts the plan when conditions change.

|Human skills|Agentic skills|Integrated collaboration|
|---|---|---|
|strategic reasoning, execution planning, priority judgment, organizational understanding, decision-making|information synthesis, dependency analysis, scenario comparison, planning support, status aggregation|The consultant translates business objectives into an executable direction while the AI agent organizes dependencies, alternatives, and progress information needed to support planning and adjustment.|

### 1.4.2. Business Problem Analysis and Requirements Definition

Business problem analysis and requirements definition identifies what business problem should be solved, determines how proposed changes may address it, and translates business needs into clear requirements that can guide implementation.

The responsibility may include analyzing complex business problems, eliciting stakeholder needs, assessing impacts on existing processes and functionality, defining functional and nonfunctional requirements, preparing use cases, documenting business rules, writing user stories, and developing validation expectations.

Agentic skills can support meeting analysis, requirement extraction, requirement drafting, classification, consistency checking, traceability, document comparison, and identification of missing information. Human skills remain essential for eliciting underlying needs, distinguishing stated requests from actual business problems, resolving conflicting stakeholder expectations, and determining whether requirements genuinely support the intended outcome.

For example, stakeholders may request a system enhancement to reduce manual work. The consultant leads discussions to understand the underlying process and discovers that different user groups define the problem differently. An AI agent organizes the discussion into candidate requirements, identifies contradictions, and compares the proposed changes with existing process documentation. The consultant returns to the stakeholders to resolve the conflicting needs, after which the agent updates the requirement artifacts and identifies remaining gaps.

|Human skills|Agentic skills|Integrated collaboration|
|---|---|---|
|business analysis, requirements elicitation, stakeholder facilitation, contextual interpretation, impact judgment|requirement extraction, user-story drafting, classification, traceability, consistency checking, document comparison|The AI agent structures and validates requirement information while the consultant identifies the real business problem, resolves ambiguity, and ensures that requirements support the intended outcome.|

### 1.4.3. Process Improvement and Root-Cause Analysis

Process improvement and root-cause analysis identifies inefficiencies, recurring problems, unnecessary complexity, and underlying causes that prevent business processes from achieving expected performance.

The responsibility may include reviewing workflows, analyzing operational data, examining performance measures, conducting root-cause analysis, identifying process gaps, assessing handoffs and dependencies, and developing opportunities to streamline or improve work.

Agentic skills can support process mapping, metric analysis, anomaly detection, repeated comparison, cause-pattern analysis, workflow comparison, and generation of improvement alternatives. Human skills remain important for understanding why existing processes developed as they did, identifying informal operating practices, distinguishing symptoms from underlying causes, and evaluating whether proposed improvements are practical.

For example, a process may repeatedly miss its expected completion time. An AI agent can analyze process data and identify that delays are concentrated around two handoffs. The consultant interviews participants and learns that one handoff frequently requires undocumented clarification. The agent then analyzes the related cases and quantifies the effect of that rework. The consultant uses the combined evidence to redesign the handoff and define a clearer process.

|Human skills|Agentic skills|Integrated collaboration|
|---|---|---|
|root-cause reasoning, process analysis, systems thinking, organizational interpretation, improvement judgment|process mapping, metric analysis, anomaly detection, workflow comparison, pattern analysis, alternative generation|The AI agent exposes recurring process patterns and potential causes while the consultant determines why those patterns occur and which improvements can be implemented effectively.|

### 1.4.4. Implementation, Change, and Project Lifecycle Coordination

Implementation, change, and project lifecycle coordination ensures that approved business changes move from initial definition through development, validation, implementation, transition, and post-implementation support.

The responsibility may involve coordinating project activities, managing requirements through the lifecycle, participating in planning and refinement sessions, monitoring dependencies, supporting validation, preparing implementation readiness, resolving issues, and supporting transition into regular operations.

Agentic skills can assist with issue tracking, requirement traceability, milestone monitoring, dependency analysis, change-impact summaries, test planning, status reporting, and identification of incomplete deliverables. Human skills remain important for coordinating cross-functional teams, managing competing priorities, resolving implementation conflicts, interpreting unexpected conditions, and determining whether the organization is ready for change.

For example, during implementation an AI agent may identify that several planned requirements have not yet been linked to validation activities. The consultant investigates and discovers that one requirement changed after an earlier stakeholder decision. The consultant coordinates the business and technical teams to confirm the revised expectation, the agent updates traceability and validation materials, and the consultant determines whether the change affects the implementation schedule.

|Human skills|Agentic skills|Integrated collaboration|
|---|---|---|
|project coordination, change judgment, stakeholder management, priority management, implementation decision-making|issue tracking, traceability, dependency analysis, milestone monitoring, validation support, status reporting|The AI agent maintains detailed execution visibility while the consultant coordinates people, priorities, changes, and decisions throughout the initiative lifecycle.|

### 1.4.5. Risk, Compliance, Governance, and Execution Control

Risk, compliance, governance, and execution control ensures that business initiatives and operational changes are performed within relevant policies, procedures, control expectations, and acceptable risk boundaries.

The responsibility may involve identifying execution risks, assessing dependencies, reviewing compliance requirements, monitoring control conditions, escalating issues, supporting governance routines, documenting decisions, and defining mitigation actions.

Agentic skills can support policy retrieval, risk identification, control mapping, issue classification, evidence organization, compliance checking, dependency monitoring, and preparation of governance materials. Human skills are particularly important for evaluating materiality, interpreting ambiguous requirements, balancing business objectives with risk considerations, determining escalation severity, and making consequential decisions.

For example, an AI agent may flag that a proposed process change conflicts with an existing control requirement. The consultant reviews the condition and determines that the control objective remains necessary but may be achieved through a different mechanism. The agent compares alternative control arrangements and organizes the evidence, while the consultant works with relevant stakeholders to determine whether the revised approach provides acceptable risk protection.

|Human skills|Agentic skills|Integrated collaboration|
|---|---|---|
|risk judgment, compliance interpretation, governance reasoning, escalation judgment, control evaluation|policy retrieval, risk identification, control comparison, compliance checking, issue tracking, evidence organization|The AI agent systematically identifies potential risk and compliance issues while the consultant determines their significance and controls how they should be mitigated or escalated.|

### 1.4.6. Business Performance Measurement and Outcome Realization

Business performance measurement and outcome realization determines whether an initiative, process change, or business execution effort is producing the intended results.

The responsibility may include establishing baseline measures, defining target outcomes, developing performance indicators, monitoring progress, analyzing results, identifying performance gaps, and determining whether expected business benefits have been realized.

Agentic skills can support metric calculation, baseline construction, trend analysis, dashboard generation, anomaly detection, progress monitoring, and comparison between expected and actual results. Human skills remain essential for defining meaningful success criteria, interpreting unexpected results, distinguishing short-term variation from structural performance changes, and determining whether corrective action is required.

For example, a process-improvement initiative may have a target to reduce processing time without increasing errors. An AI agent continuously compares current performance with the original baseline and reports that cycle time has improved but error rates have also increased. The consultant analyzes the trade-off, asks the agent to segment the errors by process stage, and identifies that the problem is concentrated in one newly changed activity. The consultant then coordinates corrective action and continues monitoring until acceptable performance is achieved.

|Human skills|Agentic skills|Integrated collaboration|
|---|---|---|
|outcome definition, performance interpretation, business judgment, benefit evaluation, corrective decision-making|baseline analysis, metric calculation, trend analysis, dashboard generation, anomaly detection, progress monitoring|The AI agent continuously measures progress and identifies deviations while the consultant determines whether the results represent successful business outcomes and what corrective action is appropriate.|

### 1.4.7. Stakeholder Coordination, Communication, and User Enablement

Stakeholder coordination, communication, and user enablement ensures that business, technical, operational, and management stakeholders remain aligned and that affected users understand and can adopt business changes.

The responsibility may include facilitating working sessions, coordinating business and technical teams, communicating decisions, preparing presentations, resolving questions, gathering user feedback, developing training, clarifying new procedures, and supporting stakeholders during transition.

Agentic skills can support meeting preparation, summarization, action-item tracking, presentation drafting, communication generation, training-material preparation, question classification, and knowledge retrieval. Human skills remain central to building trust, influencing stakeholders, facilitating difficult discussions, adjusting communication to audience needs, resolving disagreements, and understanding how people respond to organizational change.

For example, users preparing for a new process may raise a large number of questions about how different scenarios should be handled. An AI agent can classify the questions, identify recurring themes, compare them with existing requirements and procedures, and draft training material. The consultant works directly with users to clarify ambiguous cases, updates the operating guidance, and uses the agent to ensure that related materials remain consistent.

|Human skills|Agentic skills|Integrated collaboration|
|---|---|---|
|stakeholder management, facilitation, communication, influence, training, conflict resolution|meeting summarization, action tracking, presentation drafting, communication support, training-material generation, knowledge retrieval|The AI agent accelerates organization and communication of information while the consultant maintains stakeholder alignment, resolves ambiguity, and supports adoption of business changes.|

## 1.5. Changing Human and Agentic Involvement Across Business Execution Responsibilities

Human skills and agentic skills are involved throughout business execution consulting, but their relative involvement differs across responsibilities and may change during task execution.

|Responsibility|Typical human involvement|Typical agentic involvement|Typical integration|
|---|---|---|---|
|Strategic planning and business initiative execution|Very high|High|Human strategic and priority judgment supported by agentic planning, dependency analysis, and execution monitoring.|
|Business problem analysis and requirements definition|Very high|Very high|Agentic requirement organization and validation combined with human elicitation, interpretation, and business judgment.|
|Process improvement and root-cause analysis|Very high|Very high|Agentic process and metric analysis combined with human causal reasoning and improvement judgment.|
|Implementation, change, and project lifecycle coordination|Very high|Very high|Agentic tracking and traceability combined with human coordination, prioritization, and change decisions.|
|Risk, compliance, governance, and execution control|Very high|High|Agentic risk and compliance analysis combined with human materiality, escalation, and control judgment.|
|Business performance measurement and outcome realization|High|Very high|Agentic measurement and monitoring combined with human definition and interpretation of business outcomes.|
|Stakeholder coordination, communication, and user enablement|Very high|High|Agentic communication and knowledge support combined with human influence, facilitation, and adoption management.|

These involvement levels should not be interpreted as permanent allocations. A single responsibility may contain actions in which human and agentic skills compete as alternative capabilities and other actions in which they collaborate sequentially, iteratively, or recursively.

The practical objective is to construct skill configurations that use the most suitable available capabilities for each part of the work and to revise those configurations when task performance evaluation shows that another arrangement could perform more effectively or reliably.

## 1.6. Working as a Business Execution Consultant with Agentic AI

Agentic AI is becoming an increasingly powerful and promising capability for business execution consulting. Business execution consultants should therefore be prepared to accept and embrace AI agents as complementary participants in professional workflows rather than treating them only as occasional tools for documentation, research, or data analysis.

This does not reduce the importance of business execution expertise. Human professionals remain responsible for the occupation and its corresponding responsibilities. They interpret organizational objectives, understand stakeholder interests, define business problems, coordinate execution, assess risk, make implementation judgments, evaluate outcomes, influence stakeholders, and determine whether business changes are producing acceptable results.

At the same time, agentic skills can substantially expand the amount and variety of work that can be performed. AI agents can analyze large collections of business information, structure requirements, identify inconsistencies, map processes, track dependencies, monitor metrics, organize risk evidence, draft reports, prepare training materials, and continuously support execution activities at a scale difficult to achieve through direct human effort alone.

The strongest business execution practice is therefore not based on maximizing human work or maximizing agentic work independently. It is based on finding appropriate configurations of human skills and agentic skills for the responsibilities, tasks, subtasks, and actions being performed.

Where a human skill and an agentic skill can independently perform the same action, skills competition can help identify which capability is better suited to the current context. Where business initiatives require complementary capabilities, skills collaboration can activate human and agentic skills sequentially, iteratively, or recursively to achieve stronger task performance.

Task performance evaluation then determines whether the selected skills and configurations satisfy predefined expectations for requirement quality, execution effectiveness, process performance, risk control, stakeholder alignment, business-outcome realization, timeliness, and reliability. Successful capabilities can be retained and reused, while inadequate capabilities can be refined, reconfigured, combined differently, or newly developed. Reusable capabilities can subsequently contribute not only to business execution consulting but also to other occupations requiring related analytical, process, project, risk, communication, and decision-support skills.

For a business execution consultant, working effectively with agentic AI therefore means treating AI as a powerful complementary capability that can be integrated into everyday professional routines while maintaining human control of occupational responsibilities. Through appropriate skill selection, competition, collaboration, evaluation, and continuous improvement, business execution consultants and agentic AI can work together to make organizational initiatives more executable, measurable, coordinated, adaptive, and reliable.
