# 1. Working with Agentic AI: Card Access Specialist

_How human skills and agentic skills can be dynamically integrated and coordinated across card access and physical credential management responsibilities_

## 1.1. Card Access Specialist as an Occupation

A **card access specialist** is a professional who administers physical access credentials and related access-control processes to help ensure that authorized individuals receive appropriate, accurate, and secure access to organizational facilities and controlled areas. The occupation connects identity verification, badge and credential production, physical access provisioning, operational security procedures, record management, equipment use, and coordination with employees, contractors, security teams, facilities personnel, and other organizational functions.

Card access work extends beyond printing identification cards. A card access specialist must understand how identity information, authorization, access levels, credential status, physical security procedures, access-control systems, badge production processes, confidentiality requirements, and operational service expectations interact throughout the credential lifecycle.

Typical responsibilities of a card access specialist include:

- access request review and identity and authorization validation;
- credential production and personalization;
- physical access provisioning and credential lifecycle administration;
- credential quality control, security compliance, and information protection;
- credential distribution and access-service operations;
- access records, inventory, and operational documentation; and
- access issue resolution, escalation, and stakeholder coordination.

These responsibilities are fulfilled through different tasks and subtasks, and each task may require multiple actions with different skill requirements. Human skills and agentic skills can both contribute to performing these responsibilities, but their levels and forms of involvement may differ according to security requirements, request complexity, data quality, access consequences, procedural clarity, available systems, workload volume, and expected outcome.

## 1.2. Human Skills and Agentic Skills in Card Access Operations

Card access operations increasingly involve both **human skills** and **agentic skills**.

Human skills are reusable capabilities possessed and developed by card access specialists. They may include security awareness, access-control knowledge, attention to detail, identity verification, procedural judgment, equipment operation, exception handling, confidentiality, organizational skills, communication, problem solving, and professional judgment.

Agentic skills are reusable capabilities available to AI agents through their underlying models, contextual information, instructions, constraints, tools, and computational resources. In card access operations, these skills may include information extraction, request classification, record comparison, completeness checking, policy checking, workflow routing, data validation, discrepancy detection, queue prioritization, inventory tracking, document generation, reporting, and tool use.

Both skill types can contribute to the same occupational responsibilities, but their involvement levels vary. High-volume request review may involve substantial agentic support because completeness, formatting, and basic consistency can be checked systematically. Decisions involving unusual authorization conditions or conflicting information may require stronger human involvement. Credential production may combine automated data validation and workflow control with human inspection and physical handling of secure credentials.

The useful relationship is therefore not a fixed division in which certain responsibilities permanently belong to humans and others belong to AI. Human and agentic involvement can change from one task to another and even between different actions within the same task.

## 1.3. Dynamic Human–Agentic Skill Integration and Collaboration in Card Access Operations

Card access operations can be understood as an occupation in which responsibilities are fulfilled through tasks and subtasks, tasks and subtasks are executed through actions, and actions require appropriate configurations of human and agentic skills.

An **occupation** provides the overall professional context. For card access operations, this context includes administering secure physical credentials and access permissions so that authorized individuals can enter appropriate facilities and controlled spaces while unauthorized or inaccurate access is prevented.

A **responsibility** represents an expected area of professional work or an outcome that the card access specialist is expected to accomplish or maintain. Responsibilities such as validating access requests, producing credentials, administering access permissions, or maintaining credential records may each contain multiple tasks.

A **task** is a context-dependent unit of work used to fulfill part or all of a responsibility. When a task is too broad or complex for reliable execution, it may be decomposed into smaller **subtasks**.

Tasks and subtasks are executed through one or more **actions**. Actions represent reusable and configurable operations such as retrieving a request, validating identity information, comparing records, checking authorization, assigning an access profile, printing a credential, recording a serial number, inspecting a finished badge, updating credential status, preparing distribution, or escalating an exception.

Each action has **skill requirements**. These requirements describe the capabilities needed for the action to be executed effectively and reliably. Depending on the action and its context, the requirements may be satisfied by human skills, agentic skills, or an integration of both.

The skills available for card access operations reside in a **skill space** containing both human skills and agentic skills. Some skills in this space become candidate skills for executing the actions required by a particular task. A human skill in security judgment, for example, may be a candidate for resolving an unusual access request, while an agentic skill in record comparison may be a candidate for identifying discrepancies between the request and existing system information.

The candidate skills are considered relative to the requirements of the action and the occupational and task context. Appropriate skills are then selected and organized into a **skill configuration** for execution. A configuration may rely mainly on human skills, mainly on agentic skills, or on an integrated arrangement of both.

### 1.3.1. Skills Competition and Skills Collaboration

Two important relationships may occur between human skills and agentic skills when a skill configuration is constructed: **skills competition** and **skills collaboration**.

**Skills competition** occurs when both a human skill and an agentic skill can satisfy the same or similar requirements for an action, but both do not need to be applied simultaneously. Under a particular occupational and task context, one may be better suited than the other.

For example, both a card access specialist and an AI agent may be capable of checking whether a badge request contains all required fields. When the requirements are explicit and the request is structured, an agentic skill may perform the check more quickly and consistently across a large request queue. When a request contains conflicting information or depends on an unusual exception, the specialist's human judgment may provide the better fit.

The purpose of skills competition is therefore not to establish whether human or agentic skills are generally superior. It is to identify which available skill provides the better fit for a particular action under particular conditions.

**Skills collaboration** occurs when the requirements of a task or its actions cannot be satisfied effectively by one skill type alone, or when combining complementary human and agentic skills is expected to produce better task performance.

Collaboration does not require human and agentic skills to operate at exactly the same time. They may be activated at different stages of action execution in a **sequential, iterative, or recursive manner**.

For example, an AI agent may review a request and identify that the requested access level is inconsistent with the individual's recorded role. The card access specialist may investigate the discrepancy and request clarification from an authorized party. The agent may then compare the corrected information against policy rules, after which the specialist decides whether the credential can proceed to production. If another inconsistency appears, the workflow may return to an earlier validation stage.

This dynamic relationship allows card access specialists to exploit the complementary strengths of both skill types rather than relying on a permanent allocation of work.

### 1.3.2. Skill Configuration and Task Execution

A skill configuration specifies which human skills and agentic skills will be used to execute the actions associated with a task or subtask and how those skills will be coordinated.

The configuration depends on factors such as identity-data quality, request completeness, authorization clarity, security sensitivity, access consequences, procedure maturity, available systems, workload volume, service expectations, equipment condition, and intermediate results.

For one action, an agentic skill may independently satisfy the requirements. For another, a human skill may be selected instead. A more complicated access request may require several configurations across multiple actions, with human and agentic skills repeatedly interacting as the request progresses.

The configuration is therefore dynamic rather than permanent. It may change when new identity information becomes available, an access request is modified, a system record conflicts with the request, a quality check fails, or human interpretation identifies a security concern.

Throughout this process, the card access specialist remains the professional responsible for promoting and controlling the work associated with the occupation and its responsibilities. Agentic AI provides powerful complementary capabilities that can expand what the professional can validate, compare, route, track, document, monitor, and verify.

### 1.3.3. Task Performance Evaluation and Skill Space Adaptation

The performance produced through a selected skill configuration must be evaluated against **predefined evaluation metrics**. These metrics determine whether the human skills, agentic skills, or their integrated configuration are sufficiently capable of performing the relevant task and its required actions.

The evaluation criteria depend on the work being performed. In card access operations, they may include identity accuracy, credential accuracy, authorization correctness, policy compliance, processing time, record completeness, confidentiality, traceability, production quality, error rate, and service reliability.

When a selected skill or skill configuration produces performance above the defined threshold, it passes the performance evaluation and can be retained for future applications under appropriate conditions. Repeated successful use provides additional evidence about where that skill or configuration can be applied effectively.

When performance falls below the required threshold, the corresponding skill or configuration should not simply be reused without modification. Human or agentic skills may be refined or reconfigured. Existing skills may also be combined differently, or new human and agentic skills may be developed or constructed when the existing skill space does not provide an adequate capability.

For example, if an agentic skill correctly identifies incomplete badge requests but repeatedly fails to detect conflicts between badge type and access profile, its configuration may be refined with additional validation rules, better contextual information, or a required human-review stage. If a specialist lacks experience with a newly introduced credentialing procedure or access-control environment, the corresponding human skill may be developed through training and supervised practice.

Refined and newly constructed skills that satisfy the required performance criteria can be added to the skill space. Task performance evaluation therefore provides feedback not only for completing current access work but also for continuously optimizing and expanding available capabilities.

The skill space should not be constructed as an isolated collection of occupation-specific representations. Many capabilities used in card access operations are reusable across occupations. Identity verification, record comparison, workflow processing, policy checking, anomaly detection, equipment operation, confidentiality, information retrieval, communication, inventory tracking, and tool use may also contribute to security operations, identity and access management, facilities administration, human resources operations, IT service management, and other professional work.

Reusable skills can therefore be represented once and configured differently according to the responsibilities, tasks, actions, and contexts of different occupations. Occupational specialization can be introduced through security context, authorization rules, system permissions, tools, credential types, procedural constraints, and skill configurations rather than unnecessarily creating a separate skill representation whenever the same underlying capability appears elsewhere.

The resulting process is adaptive:

**occupational responsibility → task and subtask → actions → skill requirements → candidate human and agentic skills → skill configuration → task execution → task performance evaluation → skill refinement, reconfiguration, or construction → optimized and expanded skill space**

This cycle allows human and agentic capabilities to evolve together with the requirements of card access operations.

## 1.4. Essential Responsibilities of Card Access Specialist

### 1.4.1. Access Request Review and Identity and Authorization Validation

Access request review and identity and authorization validation ensures that requests for physical credentials and facility access are complete, accurate, appropriately authorized, and associated with the correct individual.

The responsibility may include reviewing requests, confirming required identity information, validating employment or contractor details, checking credential type, verifying requested access, examining photographs or identification information, and identifying missing, inconsistent, or unauthorized information.

Agentic skills can support high-volume completeness checking, field validation, record comparison, duplicate detection, policy checking, and discrepancy identification. Human skills remain essential for resolving ambiguous information, interpreting unusual access circumstances, evaluating exceptions, and determining when additional authorization is required.

For example, an AI agent may review a request and identify that the requested credential type does not match the individual's current profile. The specialist examines the underlying information and determines that the individual recently changed roles but the relevant system has not yet been updated. The specialist requests confirmation, and once corrected information is received, the agent revalidates the request before the specialist allows it to proceed.

|Human skills|Agentic skills|Integrated collaboration|
|---|---|---|
|identity verification, access-control knowledge, exception judgment, security awareness, attention to detail|completeness checking, record comparison, field validation, discrepancy detection, policy checking|The AI agent performs systematic validation across large request volumes while the specialist investigates ambiguous conditions and determines whether access is appropriately authorized.|

### 1.4.2. Credential Production and Personalization

Credential production and personalization converts an approved request into an accurate physical access credential containing the correct identity, visual, technical, and credential information.

The responsibility may include selecting the appropriate credential format, preparing credential information, operating badge-production equipment, printing or encoding cards, verifying identifiers, and ensuring that the finished credential accurately represents the approved system record.

Agentic skills can support production-data preparation, template selection, preproduction validation, serial-number capture, equipment workflow guidance, and comparison between production information and system records. Human skills remain important for operating and monitoring physical equipment, visually inspecting credentials, handling secure materials, and responding to production abnormalities.

For example, an AI agent may prepare a validated production record and select the appropriate credential template. The specialist operates the badge-production equipment and inspects the finished credential. If the photograph is misaligned or printing quality is inadequate, the specialist rejects the credential and initiates another production attempt. The agent can record the failed attempt and confirm that the replacement credential is associated with the correct record.

|Human skills|Agentic skills|Integrated collaboration|
|---|---|---|
|equipment operation, visual inspection, credential handling, production judgment, attention to detail|production-data validation, template selection, serial-number processing, record matching, workflow tracking|The AI agent prepares and verifies credential data while the specialist controls physical production, inspects quality, and ensures that secure credentials are handled correctly.|

### 1.4.3. Physical Access Provisioning and Credential Lifecycle Administration

Physical access provisioning and credential lifecycle administration ensures that credentials provide the appropriate level of access during the period in which that access is authorized.

The responsibility may include assigning access levels, applying time or location restrictions, activating credentials, modifying access permissions, replacing credentials, suspending access, revoking credentials, and closing access when authorization ends.

Agentic skills can support access-profile comparison, rule-based provisioning, lifecycle-event detection, account-status checks, permission consistency analysis, and identification of credentials requiring modification or deactivation. Human skills remain particularly important when determining whether unusual access is justified, handling sensitive exceptions, interpreting authorization boundaries, and controlling consequential access changes.

For example, an individual may transfer to another organizational function and require a different set of facility permissions. An AI agent can compare the existing access profile with the newly authorized profile and identify permissions that should be added or removed. The specialist reviews the proposed changes, verifies authorization, and approves the appropriate modification. The agent can subsequently confirm that the system state matches the approved access profile.

|Human skills|Agentic skills|Integrated collaboration|  
|access-control judgment, authorization interpretation, security reasoning, exception handling, lifecycle oversight|access-profile comparison, permission analysis, lifecycle monitoring, rule-based provisioning support, status verification|The AI agent analyzes and prepares access changes while the specialist verifies authorization and controls changes that determine where and when credentials can be used.|

### 1.4.4. Credential Quality Control, Security Compliance, and Information Protection

Credential quality control, security compliance, and information protection ensures that card access operations follow established security procedures and that credentials and sensitive information are handled accurately and securely.

The responsibility may include verifying credential information, checking physical production quality, ensuring compliance with identification and access procedures, safeguarding personal and security-related information, identifying unusual requests, and preventing the release of incorrect or unauthorized credentials.

Agentic skills can support automated consistency checks, policy comparison, anomaly detection, compliance validation, and identification of records requiring additional review. Human skills are essential for evaluating security-sensitive exceptions, protecting confidential information, recognizing suspicious circumstances, and deciding whether a credential should be withheld or escalated.

For example, an AI agent may flag a request because multiple pieces of information differ from the existing identity record. Instead of treating the differences as routine corrections, the specialist reviews the circumstances and determines that additional verification is required. Credential production is paused until the discrepancy is resolved. The agent can then recheck the corrected record against established requirements before processing resumes.

|Human skills|Agentic skills|Integrated collaboration|  
|security awareness, confidentiality, compliance judgment, anomaly interpretation, professional discretion|consistency checking, compliance validation, anomaly detection, record comparison, policy matching|The AI agent systematically identifies possible compliance and data-quality issues while the specialist evaluates security significance and controls whether processing may continue.|

### 1.4.5. Credential Distribution and Access-Service Operations

Credential distribution and access-service operations ensures that completed credentials reach the appropriate recipients through controlled processes and that credential requests are completed within expected service requirements.

The responsibility may include organizing production queues, prioritizing time-sensitive requests, preparing credentials for pickup or shipment, verifying distribution status, coordinating bulk credential activity, and supporting periods of unusually high processing demand.

Agentic skills can support queue prioritization, workload tracking, service-level monitoring, distribution-record generation, status communication, and identification of requests at risk of delay. Human skills remain important for physically handling credentials, adjusting work priorities during unusual events, verifying sensitive handoffs, and responding to operational disruptions.

For example, a large group of new personnel may require credentials within a short period. An AI agent can organize the production queue, identify complete and incomplete requests, group credentials by distribution method, and flag cases requiring intervention. The specialist concentrates human attention on exceptions and sensitive cases while allowing routine approved credentials to progress through the standard workflow.

|Human skills|Agentic skills|Integrated collaboration|  
|secure credential handling, operational prioritization, service judgment, exception management, distribution verification|queue management, prioritization support, service-level tracking, status monitoring, distribution documentation|The AI agent organizes and monitors high-volume processing while the specialist manages secure physical handling and responds to exceptions or changing operational priorities.|

### 1.4.6. Access Records, Inventory, and Operational Documentation

Access records, inventory, and operational documentation maintains accurate and traceable information about credential production, system activity, supplies, completed requests, and operational processes.

The responsibility may include recording credential identifiers, documenting production activity, maintaining access records, tracking completed requests, monitoring card and printer supplies, reconciling inventory, maintaining process documentation, and preparing operational reports.

Agentic skills can support automatic record updates, reconciliation, inventory monitoring, documentation generation, trend reporting, discrepancy detection, and prediction of supply requirements. Human skills remain necessary for validating unusual discrepancies, physically confirming inventory, maintaining procedural accuracy, and determining how documentation should change when operational processes evolve.

For example, an AI agent may compare recorded badge production with remaining credential inventory and identify an unexpected difference. The specialist performs a physical check and discovers that several credentials were consumed during equipment testing but were not recorded correctly. The specialist corrects the operational record and updates the procedure, while the agent continues reconciliation using the revised rules.

|Human skills|Agentic skills|Integrated collaboration|  
|record verification, inventory control, procedural knowledge, discrepancy investigation, documentation judgment|record reconciliation, inventory tracking, discrepancy detection, report generation, supply forecasting|The AI agent continuously organizes and reconciles operational records while the specialist verifies physical conditions, investigates exceptions, and maintains accurate procedures.|

### 1.4.7. Access Issue Resolution, Escalation, and Stakeholder Coordination

Access issue resolution, escalation, and stakeholder coordination addresses requests or operational conditions that cannot be completed through routine credential workflows.

The responsibility may include communicating request corrections, investigating access problems, resolving data discrepancies, coordinating with authorized stakeholders, escalating security-sensitive conditions, supporting credential replacement, and ensuring that unresolved issues reach the appropriate decision-maker.

Agentic skills can support issue classification, information gathering, case summarization, workflow routing, status tracking, knowledge retrieval, and identification of similar previous cases. Human skills are particularly important for communicating with affected individuals, interpreting unusual circumstances, coordinating across organizational functions, determining escalation severity, and resolving conflicts between operational needs and security requirements.

For example, a credential may be produced correctly but fail to provide expected facility access. An AI agent can compare the credential record, assigned access profile, activation status, and recent system changes and identify several possible causes. The specialist evaluates the results, communicates with the appropriate operational or security personnel, and determines whether the problem requires profile correction, credential replacement, or technical escalation. The agent can track the case and verify completion once the corrective action is performed.

|Human skills|Agentic skills|Integrated collaboration|  
|problem solving, communication, stakeholder coordination, escalation judgment, security reasoning|issue classification, case summarization, record analysis, workflow routing, status tracking|The AI agent gathers and structures evidence about access problems while the specialist coordinates stakeholders, evaluates security implications, and determines the appropriate resolution or escalation.|

## 1.5. Changing Human and Agentic Involvement Across Card Access Responsibilities

Human skills and agentic skills are involved throughout card access operations, but their relative involvement differs across responsibilities and may change during task execution.

|Responsibility|Typical human involvement|Typical agentic involvement|Typical integration|
|---|---|---|---|
|Access request review and identity and authorization validation|Very high|Very high|Agentic validation and record comparison combined with human authorization and exception judgment.|
|Credential production and personalization|Very high|High|Agentic data preparation and verification combined with human equipment operation, physical handling, and quality inspection.|
|Physical access provisioning and credential lifecycle administration|Very high|Very high|Agentic permission analysis and lifecycle monitoring governed by human authorization and security judgment.|
|Credential quality control, security compliance, and information protection|Very high|Very high|Agentic consistency and compliance checking combined with human interpretation of sensitive exceptions and risks.|
|Credential distribution and access-service operations|High|Very high|Agentic queue and service management combined with human secure handling and operational exception management.|
|Access records, inventory, and operational documentation|High|Very high|Agentic reconciliation and documentation combined with human verification of physical inventory and procedural accuracy.|
|Access issue resolution, escalation, and stakeholder coordination|Very high|High|Agentic issue analysis and tracking combined with human coordination, communication, and escalation judgment.|

These involvement levels should not be interpreted as permanent allocations. A single responsibility may contain actions in which human and agentic skills compete as alternative capabilities and other actions in which they collaborate sequentially, iteratively, or recursively.

The practical objective is to construct skill configurations that use the most suitable available capabilities for each part of the work and to revise those configurations when task performance evaluation shows that another arrangement could perform more effectively or reliably.

## 1.6. Working as a Card Access Specialist with Agentic AI

Agentic AI is becoming an increasingly powerful and promising capability for card access and physical access operations. Card access specialists should therefore be prepared to accept and embrace AI agents as complementary participants in professional workflows rather than treating them only as occasional tools for administrative processing or information retrieval.

This does not reduce the importance of card access and security expertise. Human professionals remain responsible for the occupation and its corresponding responsibilities. They interpret authorization, protect sensitive information, handle secure credentials, recognize unusual conditions, control consequential access changes, coordinate exceptions, and determine whether credential and access operations satisfy security requirements.

At the same time, agentic skills can substantially expand the amount and variety of operational information that can be processed. AI agents can review large request queues, compare identity and credential records, detect inconsistencies, monitor access-lifecycle events, reconcile production information, prioritize work, track inventory, prepare documentation, and analyze access issues at a scale difficult to achieve through direct human effort alone.

The strongest card access practice is therefore not based on maximizing human work or maximizing agentic work independently. It is based on finding appropriate configurations of human skills and agentic skills for the responsibilities, tasks, subtasks, and actions being performed.

Where a human skill and an agentic skill can independently perform the same action, skills competition can help identify which capability is better suited to the current context. Where secure and reliable access operations require complementary capabilities, skills collaboration can activate human and agentic skills sequentially, iteratively, or recursively to achieve stronger task performance.

Task performance evaluation then determines whether the selected skills and configurations satisfy predefined expectations for accuracy, authorization correctness, security compliance, confidentiality, processing reliability, service performance, and other relevant requirements. Successful capabilities can be retained and reused, while inadequate capabilities can be refined, reconfigured, combined differently, or newly developed. Reusable capabilities can subsequently contribute not only to card access operations but also to other occupations requiring identity verification, security administration, workflow processing, record management, communication, and access-control skills.

For a card access specialist, working effectively with agentic AI therefore means treating AI as a powerful complementary capability that can be integrated into everyday professional routines while maintaining human control of occupational responsibilities and security-sensitive decisions. Through appropriate skill selection, competition, collaboration, evaluation, and continuous improvement, card access specialists and agentic AI can work together to provide access operations that are more accurate, efficient, scalable, responsive, and reliable.
